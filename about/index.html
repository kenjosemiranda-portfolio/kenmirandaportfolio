About

Different rooms. The same work.

I started in government communications — managing social media for the House of Representatives, then developing public information for the Department of Finance. It taught me early that accuracy and clarity aren't in tension. In public service, you owe people both.

Since then my work has crossed four kinds of institutions. At Dr. Yanga's Colleges I led community engagement, then multimedia and marketing, and later served as Vice Dean of the College of Business Administration. I handle marketing and communications for Dr. Yanga's Hospital. Today I lead Corporate Administration Services at Golden Donuts, Inc. (Dunkin' Philippines).

Alongside that, I've served my own community in local government: as Sangguniang Kabataan chairperson of Barangay Bambang in Bocaue, Bulacan, and now as a barangay councilor chairing the committees on appropriations and on good governance, ethics and public accountability.

Running through all of it is research. I've presented studies in Osaka, Tokyo, Hong Kong and London — on how brands earn loyalty, on the conditions of contractual workers in local government, and on what makes Generation Alpha stop scrolling — and UNBOXED took me to University College Dublin. Research is how I check my instincts before I build on them.

On paper the roles look different. In practice they've asked the same three things of me: work out what the institution really needs, help people understand and care about it, and build the systems that let it last. That is what I mean by strategy, story and systems.

That combination matters more now, not less. AI can produce content, analysis and drafts faster than any team. What it can't do is choose the right problem, connect what a finance office, a classroom, a hospital and a barangay hall have in common, and then carry an idea all the way through to people acting on it. Being multi-disciplinary, thinking in systems and being able to both convey and execute the message is the value I bring.

How I think about leadership

Leadership philosophy

Leadership is not defined solely by the position one holds, but by the ability to help people and institutions move toward a common purpose.

A good program must respond to an actual need. A sound organizational system must make work easier and more accountable. And an institution must remain conscious of the people and communities it exists to serve.

How I work

  1. 01

    Ask the question first

    Research before rhetoric. I test assumptions with evidence — interviews, data, case studies — before building a strategy on them.

  2. 02

    Start from the real need

    Before planning anything, I look at how the work actually happens now and what people actually need from it.

  3. 03

    Make it understood

    Communication is part of the work, not decoration on top of it. If people can’t follow a program, it won’t hold.

  4. 04

    Build it to last

    Clear responsibilities, sensible controls and a way to monitor progress — so good initiatives outlive the people who started them.

  5. 05

    Think in systems

    Every message sits inside an institution. I look at how the parts connect — people, processes, incentives — before deciding what to change.

  6. 06

    Be exact about credit

    Most of my work is done with teams and partners. I’m specific about what was mine and what was ours.
